# Narrative Observation

Published examples from the controlled fixture:

- Visible warning: `On the sleeve, the visible writing reads: Warning: keep to the north wall.`
- No visible marking, including after removal and on the unmarked paper: `You look at the sleeve. No writing is visible there.` or `You look at the page. No writing is visible there.`
- Unresolved component: `No established object matches what you tried to examine.`
- Pronoun: `Say which object you mean.`
- Two coats: `More than one established object matches. Name the one you mean.`
- Unusable object: `You cannot see that surface from here.`

`What does my coat say?`, `I look over the markings on the cloth.`, and `Is the warning still written on my sleeve?` use the same projection. The cloth phrase matches the surface kind only when that kind is unique.

The sentences are not templates for the patrol warning. The warning appears because it is the committed visible content. A different committed string, including `The password is cedar.`, is what the sentence quotes.
