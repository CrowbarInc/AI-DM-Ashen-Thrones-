4. PLAY ONE SHORT SEQUENCE — ONLY WHILE BUDGET REMAINS
Read the actual opening. Use only the people/objects it establishes. These
examples fit the existing Cinderwatch opening; adapt names to what is visible.
Do not ask about secret facts printed in internal diagnostics.

A. NPC conversation: "I ask the tavern runner what news people are talking about."
Success: addresses the present runner and responds to that request coherently.

B. Information question: choose a concrete detail in the delivered reply, e.g.
"What does the notice say about the missing patrol?"
Success: relevant authorized information, a useful partial answer or an honest
limit. Repeating unrelated board categories is not a useful answer.

C. Follow-up: use "What do you mean by that?" only when the preceding reply has
a clear referent; otherwise ask about a named detail. If the game misread you,
correct THAT mistake naturally, e.g. "I meant the people, not the weather."
Success: retains established subject/speaker; no redundant confirmation of clear
meaning, no invented action revision, and genuine ambiguity gets a focused question.

D. Ordinary action: "I examine the notice board."
Success: supported inspection outcome, intelligible result, discovery only when
authorized; no invented travel, inventory, combat, or unearned information.

E. Continuity: refer to a real fact just learned, e.g. "What did I just learn
from the board?" Use this only if examination actually completed.
Success: consistent recall, no silent reset, no undone action or changed NPC roster.

Stop rather than manufacture a later interaction after an earlier blocker.
The sequence is coverage guidance; it is not a rigid fixed script.
