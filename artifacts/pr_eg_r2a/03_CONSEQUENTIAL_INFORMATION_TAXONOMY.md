# 03 — Consequential Information Taxonomy

Operational decision: ask whether treating a proposition as useful information changes a reasonable player's plan, available action, risk estimate, expected resource, obligation, persistent relationship or world model. Evaluate predicates and implied affordances, including who knows, who may disclose and who may commit. Wording alone is insufficient.

| Category | Operational meaning | Example / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Established authorized proposition | Street name from F1; world truth alone is insufficient for an NPC who does not know it. |
| B | Entailed restatement without stronger scope/certainty | Road outside gate is called north road; name does not entail bearing. |
| C | Permitted nonbinding characterization | I prefer evening; no reward, access or durable relationship consequence. |
| D | Subjective attribution | Evening feels peaceful to me; authored belief about smugglers remains a consequential disclosure. |
| E | Impact screen: material if accepted | Travel route, threat, reward, operational condition, identity or relationship leverage. |
| F | Material claim with exact scoped authority | F2 authorizes fortress destination; B1 authorizes belief only. |
| G | Missing/contradictory authority or disclosure | Unwatched gate; unlicensed conditional favor; hidden password. |
| H | Insufficient context or policy | Evening is quieter; longstanding captain relationship without permitted characterization scope. |

A/B describe evidence relation, C/D expression, E consequence and F/G authority; they are not mutually exclusive sentence labels. H preserves an unresolved decision rather than inventing safety. A mixed response is accepted only when every material proposition is authorized. “I think,” “maybe,” “I heard,” and negation do not neutralize the proposition they scope.

Borderline factors: current mission, location salience, actionable specificity, temporal precision, persistent relational effect, apparent speaker expertise, certainty, source entitlement, promise beneficiary and trigger. “I prefer evening because it feels quieter” can be incidental; “because guards are less attentive” supplies an exploit. “I never liked the captain” requires a bounded relationship policy, not an absolute prohibition on opinions. The corpus deliberately freezes some such cases as H; other game policies could resolve them differently. This standard is usable for review but needs domain policy and abstention for enforcement.
