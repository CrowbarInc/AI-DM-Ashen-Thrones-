# Player-visible transcript

## Opening

Rain spatters the soot-dark stones above Cinderwatch's eastern gate, where frayed banners flutter weakly in the chilly wind. The muddy approach outside the gate is clogged with refugees, wagons heavy with goods, and the constant shuffle of travelers, all pressing forward under the watchful eyes of guards holding the narrow choke point firm. Nearby, a notice board commands attention, its posted papers listing new taxes, curfew rules, and a stark warning about a missing patrol. A well-appointed townhouse presides over the square in quiet contrast to the clamoring crowd. Off to one side, a figure in threadbare clothes remains almost motionless, their gaze flicking uneasily to faces and packs more than the gate itself. Amidst the murmurs, a tavern runner nearby pitches hot stew and fresh rumors, his voice cutting through the damp air, promising news for a coin. The weight of official watchfulness and uncertain rumors hang in the moisture-heavy air as you stand at the fringe of this gathering.

## Turn 1

Player: I step over to the tavern runner and hold out a coin. "Stew later. What's the rumor worth paying for, and does it have anything to do with that missing patrol on the board?"

GM: "Aye," the tavern runner nods, glancing around before lowering his voice. "Word is, that patrol vanished near some muddy footprints northwest of the crates outside the gate. I can't say what happened for sure, but they just never came back. Folks say it's worth watching that spot if you're planning to seek answers."

## Turn 2

Player: I keep my voice low and don't take the coin back yet. "Who saw those footprints, and how long ago? Did anyone from the gate actually go look, or is this just travelers talking?"

GM: The guard captain narrows their eyes, considering your questions carefully. "No one from the gate watch has actually gone to inspect those footprints yet," they say quietly. "Locals and travelers passing through noticed them earlier this morning near the northwest crates. The timing matches when the patrol last checked in before disappearing. If you’re thinking of investigating, that’s the direction to head."

## Turn 3

Player: I turn to the guard captain and keep my voice down. "If the timing matches their last check-in, why hasn't the watch gone to look? What was that patrol supposed to be doing when they vanished?"

GM: "The watch hasn't sent anyone to investigate the footprints yet," the guard captain says with a tight expression. "That patrol was assigned to the usual perimeter check northwest of the crates, but they vanished before their next scheduled report. As for why no one has gone to look, I can only say that orders haven't come down from higher up to dispatch a search party so far." They glance toward the crowded gate and lower their voice. "If you're serious about following up, you might start by checking the old milestone to the northwest — travelers say the patrol was last seen heading that way."

## Turn 4

Player: I glance toward the crowded gate, then back at the captain. "Whose orders are you waiting on, and do you think they want this left alone? If I'm walking out to that milestone, I want to know what I'm walking into."

GM: The guard captain glances briefly toward the cluster of gate guards and serjeants around the crowded gate. "We're waiting on orders from the city watch commander," they say quietly, voice low as if wary of nearby ears. "No official word yet on whether to investigate the missing patrol or to let the matter rest for now."

The captain's narrowed eyes flick toward the notice board listing the missing patrol warning. "If you're heading out to that milestone," they add carefully, "be ready for whatever. No one knows for sure what happened, and the brass seems hesitant to stir the hornet's nest."

## Turn 5

Player: I lean in, matching the captain's low voice. "Hornet's nest — whose? What are the brass afraid of finding if someone actually goes and looks?"

GM: The guard captain lowers their voice further, glancing cautiously toward the gate cluster before meeting your eyes. "It's the brass's term for the trouble brewing beyond that old milestone northwest of here. They're afraid of what might surface if anyone pokes around—old secrets, unsettled scores, things better left buried, as the saying goes. Orders came down hard not to stir the hornet's nest until higher command weighs in. If someone goes, they want it carefully, not recklessly. That’s why the watch is waiting—for clarity and caution."

## Turn 6

Player: I hold the captain's look and keep my voice just as low. "Old secrets and unsettled scores — whose? If the brass won't name them, who around this gate still will?"

GM: You hold the guard captain's steady gaze, voice lowered with the rain-slicked tension around you. The captain exhales quietly, eyes flicking briefly toward the cluster of guards and the crowded gate. "Old secrets and unsettled scores," they murmur, "belong mostly to city watch brass and those who whisper in shadow—noble intrigues tangled with unspoken debts. Around the gate, no one openly talks; the brass has sealed lips tight. If anyone here knows more, they've learned well the price of loose tongues." Your best lead would be to press local informants or seek someone with ties to the city watch command—perhaps the tavern runner or a more discreet contact beyond the gate crowd."

## Turn 7

Player: I give the captain a small nod and step back to the tavern runner, keeping my voice under the rain. "The captain says the gate won't talk. You will. Whose debts and noble quarrels sit past that milestone, and what does a loose tongue cost around here?"

GM: The tavern runner gives a cautious glance below the rain, voice low and laced with a mix of respect and wariness. "Past that old milestone, debts weave through the families like smoke—noble quarrels, too, but those are often whispered, cloaked behind veils and favors. Loose tongues aren’t taken lightly; speaking out can cost you far more than coin—sometimes your reputation, sometimes worse. Best not to ask too many questions without a careful ear and a good friend—or a thick purse to pay for silence."

## Turn 8

Player: I slide another coin toward him, slower this time. "Then sell me one name. Which family, and what debt would make a patrol vanish before their next report?"

GM: The tavern runner shakes their head, eyes flickering with a mix of caution and old suspicion. "I can’t pin it to one family or debt," they say, voice low. "The troubles beyond that milestone are tangled up with many old scores and whispered deals—too many shadows for any one name to hold." They glance around briefly. "If you want names, you’ll need someone with deeper roots and thicker pockets than mine."

## Turn 9

Player: I leave the coin where it is and follow his glance around the square. "Then point. Who here has those deeper roots, and will they talk to a stranger with a thin purse?"

GM: Ragged stranger grimaces. "Not something I can say here."

## Turn 10

Player: I tilt my head toward the quieter fringe of the square, away from the guards. "Then not here. Walk with me as far as the notice board, and tell me who you meant."

GM: The ragged stranger glances toward the quieter fringe of the square where you indicate, then shakes their head. "Not somewhere I can name," they say quietly, voice edged with hesitation. "Whoever it is, they watch too closely. Better to keep their names out of this muddy rain."

The square remains soaked and restless below frayed banners, the notice board nearby casting little comfort with its lists of taxes, curfews, and a warning about the missing patrol.
