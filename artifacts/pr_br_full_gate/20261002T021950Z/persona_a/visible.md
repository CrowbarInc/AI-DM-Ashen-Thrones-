# Player-visible transcript

## Opening

Rain spatters against soot-dark stone as frayed banners flutter weakly above the eastern gate of Cinderwatch. The muddy approach is crowded with refugees, wagons, and twisting foot traffic, all bottlenecked where the guards hold firm in a tense choke point. Nearby, a gate serjeant maintains order, his sharp eyes flicking between the restless crowd and the large notice board that displays new taxes, curfew rules, and a stark warning about a missing patrol. A well-dressed townhouse overlooks the scene, its noble colors a quiet contrast to the clamoring masses below. Alongside the crowd, a tavern runner shouts offers of hot stew and paid rumor, weaving eagerly through the throng. Amid them stands a lone figure in threadbare clothes, unnervingly still, watching packs and people rather than the line itself, as if waiting for something unspoken to unfold.

## Turn 1

Player: I step out of the crush and read the notice board from top to bottom—the new taxes, the curfew, and the warning about the missing patrol.

GM: You step aside from the jostling crowd to inspect the notice board carefully. The upper section details new taxes imposed by the Ash Compact authorities—the levies on trade goods, livestock, and caravan tolls have increased notably, a strain on merchants and travelers alike. Beneath that, the curfew is outlined: from dusk till dawn, movement within the gate district is restricted, enforced strictly amid rising unrest. The starkest message is the warning posted at the bottom, cautioning about a missing patrol of Cinderwatch guards last seen heading northwest along a muddy track near the stacked crates. Local rumors hint that this patrol may have fallen victim to bandits or worse, though no confirmation has surfaced. The gate serjeant nearby keeps a watchful eye on a roster, his expression hardened by the weight of these worries.

## Turn 2

Player: I walk up to the gate serjeant and ask what a lone traveler owes to pass, and whether the missing patrol changes who gets through.

GM: "A lone traveler must present themselves at the gate and submit to the standard tolls and examinations," the gate serjeant explains, eyes flicking briefly toward the notice board. "That hasn't changed, but with the patrol missing—the one usually passing through this choke—you'll find their usual route altered since the Ash Compact's census choke tightened."

They glance toward the northwest mud track past the stacked crates. "The patrol took that path last, though we don't know their current fate. It doesn't change who may pass, but expect closer scrutiny and delays while replacements are marshaled."

## Turn 3

Player: I step up to the gate and tell the serjeant I'll take the standard toll and examination now.

GM: The gate serjeant nods, stepping aside to mark your passage on the roster board. "Very well, the standard toll and examination it is," they say briskly, signaling a tired guard to step forward and see to the process. Around you, the muddy crowd shifts uneasily beneath the chill rain as the scrutiny begins. The gate's frustration hums low—delays stretch longer with the missing patrol and tightened curb on all travelers. Ahead, you can glimpse the gate captain watching the procedure with an unreadable expression.

## Turn 4

Player: I face the guard, give my name as Galinor, a traveling wizard with no goods to declare, and wait for them to name the toll and begin the check.

GM: The gate serjeant meets your gaze steadily as you identify yourself as Galinor, a traveling wizard without goods to declare. After a brief pause, they state the toll for such travelers is a modest fee and gesture to the nearby gate guard to begin the routine check. The guard moves forward, preparing to inspect your belongings with practiced efficiency.

## Turn 5

Player: I set my pack open for the guard and ask the exact coin of that modest fee so I can pay it while they look.

GM: The gate serjeant eyes your open pack briefly before replying, "The modest fee for a lone traveler passing through is exactly three silver coins. Payment is expected upfront, especially with the delays tightening the checkpoint." They nod toward the tired guard ready to inspect your belongings, signaling the toll must be settled before proceeding.

## Turn 6

Player: I count out three silver and hand them to the serjeant, then stand clear so the guard can finish inspecting the open pack.

GM: "The gate serjeant nods as you count out the three silver and accept the coins with a curt, practiced motion. "Thank you, traveler," they say. "We'll be through shortly; the guard will make their inspection." The gate guard steps forward, fingers already probing quietly through the folds of your open pack with swift efficiency, eyes sharp despite the rain and fatigue."

## Turn 7

Player: I keep my hands in sight and let the guard finish the pack, then ask if I'm clear to go through.

GM: The gate guard, still carefully finishing their inspection of your open pack, looks up and nods. "You're clear to pass," they say shortly, though their eyes flick to the snarled traffic and the notice board where the warning about the missing patrol still hangs. "Keep to the main road and stay within curfew." The gate serjeant nearby stays alert, watching the shifting crowd as the muddy line inches forward past the gate.

## Turn 8

Player: I close my pack, thank them, and walk through the gate onto the main road.

GM: You carefully close your pack, nod to the gate guard and serjeant in thanks, and step through the rusty iron gate onto the muddied main road beyond. The rain patters steadily as you leave the choke point behind, stepping toward the bustling sprawl of Cinderwatch beyond the eastern gate district. The sounds of town life swell ahead — a mixture of clattering wagon wheels, distant shouted conversations, and the occasional clink of coin. The gate remains busy behind you, a reminder of the ever-present tension and watchfulness lingering at this frontier outpost.

## Turn 9

Player: I stay on the main road and ask the first local I pass where a traveler can get a bed and a meal before curfew.

GM: "A tavern runner, wiping rain from their brow as they ward off the drizzle near a steaming barrel of stew, glances up when you approach. "If you're a traveler looking for shelter and a meal before curfew," they say, voice low and hurried, "the Rusty Tankard down the road is your best bet. They offer beds and a hot meal, but you'll want to get in before sundown." They gesture down the main road where a weathered sign points toward the tavern's murky lantern-lit entrance. "After that, the curfew tightens and the gates shut. It's not wise to be caught outside."

## Turn 10

Player: I follow the weathered sign down the main road and go into the Rusty Tankard.

GM: You step off the muddy main road, following the weathered wooden sign bearing the faded name "Rusty Tankard." A low building with soot-darkened stone walls and a sagging roof stands before you. The scent of spilled ale and worn leather blends with the damp air as you push open the heavy door and step inside, greeted by murmurs and the glint of hearth fires within.
