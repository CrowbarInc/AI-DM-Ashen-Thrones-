ASHEN THRONES — ATGB / PRODUCT REALIZATION
PR-PLAY-72: CONSEQUENCE-AWARE PLAYER INITIATIVE

MISSION
Demonstrate that Ashen Thrones can understand and
respond to naturally expressed player intentions
while preserving authoritative gameplay state.

The player should not need to select a predefined
button or know an internal command name to perform
an otherwise supported action.

The engine must distinguish between:

1. What the player wants to accomplish.
2. What the character is attempting.
3. What the current world state permits.
4. What the authoritative mechanics resolve.
5. What the player is allowed to learn.
6. What the AI-DM may narrate.

The AI may help interpret intent.

The AI may help present the outcome.

The AI must not independently invent or commit
authoritative consequences.

PRIMARY QUESTION

CAN A PLAYER EXPRESS A REASONABLE INTENTION IN
ORDINARY LANGUAGE AND RECEIVE A CONTEXT-AWARE,
AUTHORITATIVE RESPONSE THAT REFLECTS THEIR
PREVIOUS DECISIONS?

SECONDARY QUESTION

CAN THE ENGINE DISTINGUISH BETWEEN:

- Supported intentions.
- Ambiguous intentions.
- Unsupported intentions.
- Impossible intentions.
- Information requests.
- Attempts to perform actions.
- Attempts to obtain hidden information.

WITHOUT REDUCING ALL OF THEM TO GENERIC REFUSALS?

THIS IS A GAMEPLAY-CAPABILITY EXPANSION CYCLE.

It is not a general narration-polishing cycle.

It is not a campaign-generation redesign.

It is not a broad AI-agent architecture rewrite.

It is not an unrestricted natural-language
world-generation experiment.

Do not commit or push without explicit authorization.

3. PLAYER INITIATIVE CONTRACT
======================================================================

Define a clear contract for natural intentions.

An interpreted intention should contain
only the information necessary to resolve
a supported action.

Conceptual fields:

- Intent category.
- Requested action.
- Referenced target.
- Current scene.
- Relevant authoritative context.
- Interpretation confidence.
- Ambiguity status.
- Required clarification.
- Supported action mapping.

Use existing schemas where possible.

Do not introduce a new schema merely because
the conceptual model above lists fields.

The interpreter may propose an action.

The interpreter may not commit state.

The interpreter may not invent world facts.

The interpreter may not invent new inventory.

The interpreter may not reveal hidden information.

The interpreter may not bypass prerequisites.

The authoritative resolver remains responsible
for deciding whether the action succeeds.

The publication layer remains responsible
for deciding what the player may learn.

======================================================================
4. DISTINGUISH INTENT FROM OUTCOME
======================================================================

The engine must distinguish:

PLAYER INTENTION:

"I want to find out whether the guards
are searching for the patrol."

from:

AUTHORITATIVE OUTCOME:

"The guards have begun searching."

The first is a request.

The second is a world-state claim.

The interpreter must not transform the first
into the second without authoritative support.

Likewise:

PLAYER INTENTION:

"I want to follow the footprints."

does not automatically mean:

"The footprints lead to a hidden camp."

The engine must determine:

1. Whether the footprints are known.

2. Whether they are currently accessible.

3. Whether following them is supported.

4. Whether prerequisites are satisfied.

5. Whether a relevant action exists.

6. Whether resolution changes state.

7. What information can be published.

If the action is not supported:

The engine should explain the limitation
without fabricating a successful outcome.

Where an existing supported alternative exists,
the engine may suggest it.

Do not default to a generic refusal when
a narrower, useful response is possible.
