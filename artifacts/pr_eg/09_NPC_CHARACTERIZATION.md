# 09 — NPC Characterization

## Used when present

The authority record copies `role` and `disposition` from the NPC, and the speaker name from the social payload. The prompt already addresses the selected speaker. No biography was added.

Sergeant Mara in the disposable scene is a guard, disposition neutral, with the street topic and the visible lines “watches the road” and “looks concerned.” The concerned line is not spoken by this cycle.

## Incidental characterization

For an NPC with no authored personal topics, a personal or opinion question may improvise one non-canonical remark. The live remark was “It’s this watch, mostly.” That is consistent with a guard on watch. It does not add a motive, a faction, or a schedule.

Porter Edric is in the frozen holdouts as a background preference question. The deterministic classification permits improvisation and does not invent a fact. He was not in the live conversation.

## Consistency

The determined lines are the same two sentences for every NPC, with the speaker’s name in front. They do not differentiate Mara from another guard except by name. A recovered quote can differ. Nothing stops a later turn from revising that commitment when the subject matches.

## Limitations

This is not a personality engine. Ordinary NPCs do not gain traits, relationships, or goals. The live preference question did not sound like a person choosing morning or evening. The live trouble question did, once.
