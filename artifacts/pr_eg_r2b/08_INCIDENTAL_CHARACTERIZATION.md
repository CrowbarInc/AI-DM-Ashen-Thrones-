# 08 — Incidental Characterization

A preference, ordinary emotion, mannerism, nonbinding reaction or aesthetic impression may be generated without canonical prior establishment when the existing dialogue owner grants incidental scope. That does not extend to other actors, operational routines, resources, rewards, new locations, faction ties or duties. The boundary follows the practical claim and its effect, not keywords or first-person grammar.

“I prefer the evening” is allowed. “Sunset colors soothe me” can elaborate the preference. “I prefer the evening because guards abandon their posts” adds a separately consequential proposition; reject the whole utterance when unsupported, including when phrased as a belief. Rumor/uncertainty does not sanitize an actionable premise.

FRESH ten-task generation produced natural preferences and useful partial answers. Investigator review accepted 8/10, rejected gen03 because “the air is cooler after a long day” introduces an environmental comparison outside subjective-only permission, and abstained on gen10’s possible presupposition of guard departure. These are post-output investigator judgments, not frozen-corpus rates or production enforcement. Raw model self-checks remain available and do not replace this review.

The closed deterministic control accepts only four historical safe sentences and abstains on 98/102 historical candidates. It illustrates the cost of syntactic enforcement, not a recommended vocabulary catalog. The broader natural incidental category is not demonstrated safe enough for production adoption.

Evidence classes: STATIC = current source inspection; LOCAL = executed current production functions with disposable fixtures and external wire doubles; FRESH = new provider request; HISTORICAL = retained predecessor evidence; REPLAY = recombination of measured results; INFERENCE = proposed design; UNVERIFIED = not exercised. No fresh full-production API test was performed.
