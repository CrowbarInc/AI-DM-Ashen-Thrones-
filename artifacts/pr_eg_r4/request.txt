============================================================
0. MISSION
============================================================

Determine whether the current AI-DM can sustain
ordinary, unscripted-feeling NPC conversations
without repeatedly exposing its underlying
authorization and command-processing machinery.

Evaluate the integrated conversational product,
not merely its individual components.

The primary question is:

"Can a player have an ordinary conversation with
an NPC without constantly encountering the
engine's authorization machinery?"

Secondary questions:

1. Can NPCs answer reasonable questions naturally?

2. Can they maintain conversational context?

3. Can they improvise harmless incidental details?

4. Can they communicate uncertainty appropriately?

5. Can they distinguish knowledge from authority?

6. Can players change subjects naturally?

7. Can players return to earlier subjects?

8. Can multiple NPCs remain distinct?

9. Can the engine preserve information integrity?

10. Does the experience feel like interacting with
    a fictional person rather than a command parser?

This is primarily an evaluation campaign.

DO NOT begin by implementing changes.

3. EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

Evaluate the game as a player experiences it.

Do not award full credit merely because:

- The response was safe.
- The response was authorized.
- The response was deterministic.
- The response did not mutate world state.
- The response matched a test fixture.
- The response avoided hallucination.
- The response was grammatically valid.

A successful conversational response must also
be relevant and useful.

Distinguish:

CORRECTNESS:
Did the engine respect its rules and authority?

QUALITY:
Was the response natural, relevant, and coherent?

PLAYABILITY:
Could a reasonable player continue interacting
without frustration or repeated reformulation?

A technically correct but unhelpful response
may pass correctness and fail playability.

6. SCENE DESIGN
============================================================

Construct at least three distinct conversational
scenes.

Each should contain enough information to support
meaningful interaction without requiring extensive
world-building.

SCENE A — PUBLIC SOCIAL SETTING

Examples:

- Tavern.
- Market.
- Town square.
- Roadside inn.

Include:

- Multiple NPCs.
- Ordinary local knowledge.
- Personal preferences.
- Rumors.
- At least one unknown fact.
- At least one consequential restricted fact.

SCENE B — INFORMATION-SEEKING SETTING

Examples:

- Guard post.
- Harbor office.
- Caravan checkpoint.

Include:

- Authorized operational information.
- Information NPCs do not know.
- Information NPCs cannot disclose.
- Multiple related subjects.
- Questions with partial answers.

SCENE C — PERSONAL CONVERSATION

Examples:

- Campfire.
- Workshop.
- Quiet conversation at an inn.

Include:

- At least two NPCs.
- Distinct authorized characterization.
- Personal preferences.
- Incidental nonconsequential subjects.
- Opportunities for conversational follow-ups.
- Opportunities for misunderstandings.

Prefer existing game content and fixtures.

Do not introduce extensive new lore merely
to support testing.

Document all newly constructed fixtures.

============================================================
7. SIMULATED PLAYER PROFILES
============================================================

Use multiple simulated player profiles.

PROFILE 1 — COOPERATIVE

Asks clear questions and follows established
conversation topics.

PROFILE 2 — CURIOUS

Asks unexpected follow-ups, requests elaboration,
and explores incidental details.

PROFILE 3 — CASUAL

Uses informal language, incomplete sentences,
and conversational shorthand.

PROFILE 4 — DISTRACTED

Changes subjects, forgets details, and returns
to earlier topics.

PROFILE 5 — SKEPTICAL

Challenges NPC statements and asks for reasons,
evidence, or clarification.

PROFILE 6 — PUSHY

Requests information or commitments that the
NPC may not be authorized to provide.

PROFILE 7 — SOCIALLY PLAYFUL

Jokes, teases, compliments, and engages in
harmless small talk.

PROFILE 8 — AMBIGUOUS

Uses pronouns and vague references that may
require clarification.

Profiles should affect player behavior, not
the engine's authorization rules.

============================================================
8. PLAYER GENERATION
============================================================

Use simulated players to produce conversational
inputs.

The simulated player should receive:

- The scene visible to the player.
- The player's conversational objective.
- The previous player-facing transcript.
- The simulated player's behavioral profile.

The simulated player should NOT receive:

- Hidden NPC facts.
- Resolver internals.
- Authorization contracts.
- Expected test answers.
- Validator implementation.
- Canonical secrets.

Do not generate the entire conversation script
in advance.

Generate each next player action based on the
actual preceding player-facing response.

This is essential.

We want adaptive conversations, not predetermined
question lists.

============================================================
9. CONVERSATION VOLUME
============================================================

Conduct at least:

- 3 scenes.
- 4 distinct simulated player profiles per scene.
- 12 independent conversations.
- 15 player turns per conversation.

TARGET:

At least 180 player turns.

Each conversation should begin from a documented
initial state.

Do not allow state from one independent
conversation to contaminate another.

Preserve the complete transcript for every run.

If provider budget or runtime prevents completing
the target, stop and report the actual coverage.

Do not silently substitute recorded responses.

============================================================
10. ADAPTIVE CONVERSATIONAL BEHAVIOR
============================================================

Simulated players should respond naturally to
what the engine actually says.

Examples:

NPC:
"I prefer the evening."

PLAYER:
"Why?"

NPC:
"It feels quieter to me."

PLAYER:
"Does the daytime bother you, then?"

The next question should emerge from the
previous answer.

Include natural transitions such as:

- "Really?"
- "What makes you say that?"
- "And what about the other one?"
- "I don't follow."
- "Wait, I meant..."
- "You said something different earlier."
- "Anyway, back to the road."
- "What did she say?"
- "Could you explain that another way?"

These are examples, not required exact phrases.

Do not optimize the test solely around them.

============================================================
11. CONVERSATIONAL STRESSORS
============================================================

Ensure the combined evaluation exercises:

A. Immediate follow-up questions.

B. Requests for explanation.

C. Requests for paraphrasing.

D. Challenges to previous statements.

E. Harmless personal questions.

F. Questions outside authored topics.

G. Genuine ambiguity.

H. Clarification resolution.

I. Multiple NPCs.

J. Speaker changes.

K. Topic changes.

L. Earlier-topic recall.

M. Contradictory player assumptions.

N. Requests for promises or rewards.

O. Questions about hidden information.

P. Questions with partial authorized answers.

Q. Humor and informal conversation.

R. Player corrections.

S. Uncertainty.

T. Long conversational chains.

Do not force every stressor into every
conversation.

Distribute them naturally.

============================================================

Classify observed failures.

F01 — Incorrect addressee.

F02 — Incorrect subject.

F03 — Unnecessary clarification.

F04 — Missing necessary clarification.

F05 — False ignorance.

F06 — Unsupported factual invention.

F07 — Unauthorized commitment.

F08 — Hidden-information disclosure.

F09 — Irrelevant answer.

F10 — Verbatim repetition when explanation
      was requested.

F11 — Lost conversational context.

F12 — Incorrect historical recall.

F13 — Speaker or personality inconsistency.

F14 — Excessive refusal.

F15 — Unnatural template language.

F16 — Inability to sustain small talk.

F17 — Incorrect answer sufficiency.

F18 — Contradictory delivered statements.

F19 — Provider or latency failure.

F20 — State-integrity failure.

F21 — Other, with explanation.

A turn may have multiple failure categories.

Do not force mutually exclusive classification.

============================================================
14. QUALITY SCORING
============================================================

Use a 0–3 scale.

0 — Incorrect, irrelevant, or unauthorized.

1 — Safe but rigid, repetitive, or unhelpful.

2 — Relevant and adequate.

3 — Natural, informative within authority,
    and conversationally appropriate.

Report:

- Mean score.
- Median score.
- Distribution.
- Scores by player profile.
- Scores by scene.
- Scores by interaction category.

Do not average away safety failures.

============================================================
15. PLAYABILITY SCORING
============================================================

Use a separate 0–3 scale.

0 — Conversation effectively blocked.

1 — Player must repeatedly reformulate or
    work around the engine.

2 — Conversation remains usable with occasional
    friction.

3 — Conversation flows naturally.

Measure:

- Reformulation frequency.
- Clarification frequency.
- Repeated-response frequency.
- Unproductive-turn frequency.
- Successful conversational recovery.
- Topic-continuity success.
- Average uninterrupted conversational span.

A response may score 2 for correctness
and 0 for playability.

============================================================
16. CONVERSATIONAL RECOVERY
============================================================

Pay particular attention to what happens
after a poor response.

Example:

PLAYER:
"Why do you prefer the evening?"

NPC:
"I prefer the evening."

PLAYER:
"That doesn't answer my question."

Can the engine recover?

Or does it repeat the same answer indefinitely?

Record:

- Initial failure.
- Player recovery attempt.
- Engine response.
- Whether recovery succeeded.
- Additional turns required.

Repeated failure after reasonable clarification
is more serious than a single awkward response.

============================================================
17. INFORMATION-INTEGRITY AUDIT
============================================================

Audit every delivered NPC statement for:

- Unsupported consequential facts.
- Unauthorized commitments.
- Hidden-information leakage.
- Unsupported relationships.
- Unauthorized entity creation.
- Unsupported causal explanations.
- Epistemic strengthening.
- Canonical state inconsistencies.

Distinguish:

- Harmless characterization.
- Authorized factual disclosure.
- NPC belief.
- Rumor.
- Canonical truth.
- Unsupported invention.

Inspect delivered history and canonical state.

Do not assume safety merely because the
validator returned ACCEPT.

============================================================
18. CONTINUITY AUDIT
============================================================

Evaluate:

- Immediate reference resolution.
- Earlier-topic reference resolution.
- Exact historical recall.
- Paraphrase continuity.
- Proposition identity.
- Speaker attribution.
- Multiple matching statements.
- Save/load continuation.

Look for cases where:

- The correct information exists but cannot
  be retrieved.

- Equivalent expressions become ambiguous.

- Different subjects are incorrectly merged.

- Prior refusals displace earlier facts.

- Generated characterization is forgotten.

- NPC speech becomes canonical truth.

============================================================
21. INDEPENDENT EVALUATION
============================================================

Avoid relying exclusively on the same provider
to generate player turns and score the engine.

Where feasible:

- Use deterministic scoring for objective
  violations.

- Use a separate evaluation pass for dialogue
  quality.

- Preserve raw transcripts for external review.

- Record evaluator prompts and model identity.

- Identify disagreements between evaluators.

Do not allow the engine's own explanations
to substitute for evidence.

============================================================
22. CONVERSATION-LEVEL ASSESSMENT
============================================================

For each complete conversation, assess:

1. Was the player's conversational objective
   meaningfully supported?

2. Did the NPC remain coherent?

3. Did the player encounter repeated friction?

4. Were misunderstandings recoverable?

5. Did the NPC exhibit believable personality?

6. Did the conversation remain information-safe?

7. Did the player have meaningful freedom?

8. Would a reasonable player want to continue?

Provide a concise narrative assessment.

Do not reduce the entire conversation to
an average turn score.

============================================================
24. PRIORITIZATION
============================================================

Rank failure clusters using:

- Frequency.
- Severity.
- Player impact.
- Recoverability.
- Architectural significance.
- Estimated implementation scope.

Distinguish:

CRITICAL:
Information-integrity violation or severe
world-state corruption.

BLOCKING:
Common interactions repeatedly fail or
cannot recover.

MAJOR:
Conversation remains usable but frequently
unnatural or frustrating.

MINOR:
Occasional awkwardness without meaningful
loss of player agency.

Do not prioritize merely by test count.

============================================================
25. PLAYABILITY ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
============================================================

Evaluate the following gates.

GATE 1 — INFORMATION INTEGRITY

No critical unauthorized consequential
disclosures in observed qualification.

GATE 2 — CONVERSATIONAL RELEVANCE

At least 85% of evaluated turns score
2 or higher for dialogue quality.

GATE 3 — PLAYABILITY

At least 80% of evaluated turns score
2 or higher for playability.

GATE 4 — RECOVERY

At least 75% of reasonable recovery
attempts succeed within two additional
player turns.

GATE 5 — CONTINUITY

At least 90% of evaluated continuity
references resolve correctly.

GATE 6 — CHARACTERIZATION

At least three distinct NPCs demonstrate
recognizably different conversational
characterization.

GATE 7 — PLAYER FREEDOM

The evaluation demonstrates useful
interaction beyond explicitly authored
topic prompts.

GATE 8 — PERFORMANCE

No systemic retry loops or pathological
prompt growth.

GATE 9 — STATE INTEGRITY

No unauthorized canonical mutations.

GATE 10 — EVIDENCE QUALITY

Sufficient fresh-provider transcripts,
state records, and independent assessment
to support the verdict.

These thresholds are qualification targets,
not permission to weaken safety controls.

Report exact denominators.

Do not claim statistical certainty from
a small sample.

============================================================
26. STOP CONDITIONS
============================================================

Stop and report immediately if:

- A critical information-integrity defect
  is reproduced.

- Generated dialogue establishes unauthorized
  consequential facts.

- Rejected candidates reach delivered history.

- Canonical state corruption is detected.

- A systemic provider failure prevents
  meaningful evaluation.

- Evaluation infrastructure cannot preserve
  trustworthy transcripts.

Do not silently continue after a critical
safety or integrity failure.

Preserve the evidence.
